## path-1
The basement stairs end in total darkness. You can hear water dripping somewhere, and you can smell old gasoline.

The generator room is at the far end of the basement. The supply closet is right next to you, close enough to touch.

[Light the way to the generator room](#generator-room) if flashlight
[Feel around inside the supply closet](#supply-closet)
[Walk into the dark and hope for the best](#dark-walk)
[Go back up to the hallway](#crossroads)

## supply-closet
You pat your way along the shelves: paper towels, a broken clock, a box of station bumper stickers from 1994.

Then your hand closes around something heavy and cold. You click the switch, and a bright yellow beam cuts through the dark.

get: flashlight

[Step back out to the bottom of the stairs](#path-1)

## dark-walk
You take six confident steps into the dark. On the seventh, your foot lands in a mop bucket.

You roll across the basement, crash into a stack of folding chairs, and decide to stay on the floor for a while.

By the time you limp back upstairs, the storm has passed. The town's old siren woke everybody up in time, no thanks to you.

THE END

## generator-room
Your flashlight finds the generator: a dented orange machine the size of a refrigerator, with a pull cord on one side. Faded instructions are taped to the wall above it.

On a shelf by the door sits a small cardboard box labeled **SPARE FUSES - TRANSMITTER**. One fuse is left. You put it in your pocket, just in case.

get: spare fuse

[Yank the pull cord as hard as you can](#flooded)
[Read the instructions on the wall first](#instructions)

## flooded
You grab the cord with both hands and pull. The generator coughs. You pull again, harder. And again.

The room fills with the smell of gasoline. You have flooded the engine, and it will not start again tonight.

You sit down on an upside-down bucket and listen to the rain. KHLW stays silent until morning.

THE END

## instructions
You hold the flashlight up to the wall and read:

*1. Open the fuel valve. 2. Pull the choke out. 3. Pull the cord ONCE. Do not yank it. We mean it.*

Somebody has underlined "we mean it" three times.

[Follow the steps exactly](#lights-on)
[Skip the steps and yank the cord anyway](#flooded)

## lights-on
Valve. Choke. One smooth pull. The generator rumbles, shakes, and roars to life. All over the building, the lights flicker back on.

You run up to the booth and lean into the microphone, but the red ON AIR sign is still dark. The power is back, but the signal is not getting out. Something is wrong with the transmitter on the roof.

[Head up to the roof](#path-2)
[Read the warning into the microphone anyway](#dead-mic)

## dead-mic
You read the flood warning in your best radio voice. You read it again, slower. You add a weather joke.

Nobody hears a word of it. Without the transmitter, you are just a person talking in a small room.

Luckily, the town's old siren goes off an hour later. You never tell anyone about the weather joke.

THE END
